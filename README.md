## Live Engagement: Idowu Ajiri Foundation

**Real organization. Real regulatory stakes. Active program.**

The Idowu Ajiri Foundation is a dual-jurisdiction nonprofit. The Nigerian entity operates under CAC registration with active SCUML and AML/CFT obligations. The US entity is in pre-operational formation under IRS 501(c)(3). Both jurisdictions are covered proactively: compliance readiness was built before full US operational activity begins.

Nonprofits operating across US and Nigerian regulatory regimes face different, sometimes conflicting obligations in AML/CFT, data privacy, and fund management. Each jurisdiction has its own regulator, its own filing calendar, and its own sanctions exposure. The program addresses both simultaneously rather than treating them as separate compliance tracks.

My role is Compliance Officer. The conflict-of-interest disclosure memo in the repository is in my name.

**[View the full IAF GRC Program repository](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/idowu-ajiri-grc-program)**

## Case Study: Emyzer Technology and Emyzer Nexus

**Fictional environment. Platform-implemented. Audit-ready documentation.**

This case study models a governance problem that GRC teams face regularly: an acquisition closes and the acquired entity has no formal compliance program. The question is how you establish governance authority, classify inherited risk, and integrate documentation across two entities from Day 0.

Phase 1 built the GRC baseline for Emyzer Technology before the acquisition. Phase 2 shows what happened after Emyzer Technology acquired Veridian AI, an AI-native startup with two High-Risk AI systems and no GRC program. Both phases were built and implemented in ServiceNow GRC.

---

### Phase 2: Emyzer Nexus (Post-Acquisition Parent)

Veridian AI had two AI products at acquisition close: a predictive customer churn model and an LLM-assisted contract review tool. Both were classified as High-Risk under EU AI Act Annex III. Full conformity obligations under Articles 9-15 applied from the moment Emyzer Nexus became the legal operator.

| Artifact | What it resolves |
|---|---|
| [M&A GRC Integration Charter](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/integration-management/m%26a-grc-integration-charter.md) | Veridian had no policies at acquisition close. This charter establishes which policies govern the acquired entity from Day 0, which governance body has authority to accept inherited risk, and what conditions trigger a policy review or retirement. Includes a 15-entry integration risk register with owners and target resolution dates. |
| [Policy Rationalization Roadmap](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/integration-management/rationalization-roadmap.md) | Phase 1 policies were written for Emyzer Technology as a standalone entity. ### Phase 1: Emyzer Technology (Foundational GRC)

Phase 1 established the governance baseline that Phase 2 depends on. All artifacts remain in effect as subsidiary documentation under the Emyzer Nexus policy hierarchy.

**Security Policies**

| Policy | Key design decision | Frameworks |
|---|---|---|
| [Information Security Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/information-security-policy.md) | Parent policy governing the ISMS. Establishes the Security Steering Committee, exception escalation path, and the review cadence that all other policies inherit. | NIST CSF 2.0, ISO 27001:2022, COBIT |
| [Access Control Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/access-control-policy.md) | Role-based access with privileged access controls and access review attestation requirements. At Phase 2, the AI Governance Committee is added as an access authority for AI system records. | NIST SP 800-53, ISO 27001:2022 A.5.15-A.5.18, COBIT DSS05 |
| [Incident Management Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/incident-management-policy.md) | Defines a 4-hour initial assessment SLA and the 72-hour GDPR notification obligation. IRT activation criteria are set at classification, not escalation, to avoid the delay that commonly causes missed notification windows. | NIST SP 800-61, ISO 27035, GDPR Art. 33-34 |
| [Risk Management Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/risk-management-policy.md) | Sets the 5x5 hybrid scoring matrix applied in all Phase 1 and Phase 2 risk assessments. Defines the materiality threshold above which Risk Management Committee formal acceptance is required, so risk acceptance decisions are governed, not informal. | ISO 31000:2018, NIST CSF 2.0, COSO ERM |
| [BC/DR Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/bcdr-policy.md) | Built on a 42-stakeholder BIA with 4-tier process prioritization, RTO/RPO targets per tier, and financial exposure quantification across 12 critical processes. Recovery objectives are grounded in actual operational data, not estimates. | ISO 22301:2019, NIST SP 800-34 |
| [Change Management and Configuration Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/change-management-and-configuration-policy.md) | Three-tier change classification with defined CAB authority limits. At Phase 2, AI system material changes are escalated to the AI Governance Committee rather than handled through standard CAB review. | ITIL 4, ISO 27001:2022, COBIT BAI06 |
| [Acceptable Use Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/acceptable-use-policy.md) | Covers personal device use, shadow IT, and staff use of AI tools. The AI tool provisions were written with the Veridian AI acquisition context in mind. | ISO 27001:2022 A.5.10, NIST CSF |
| [Data Classification Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/data-classification-policy.md) | Four-tier classification (Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted) with handling, labeling, and disposal requirements per tier. At Phase 2, AI training data is classified under this scheme and subject to additional GDPR controls. | NIST SP 800-60, ISO 27001:2022 A.5.12-A.5.13, GDPR |
| [Third-Party Risk Management Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/third-party-risk-management-policy.md) | Risk tiering criteria for vendors with review cadences by tier. Extended at Phase 2 with the AI Vendor Risk Due Diligence Addendum when inherited Veridian AI vendor relationships required controls the base policy did not cover. | ISO 27036, NIST SP 800-161, NIST CSF ID.SC |
| [Security Awareness and Training Policy](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/blob/main/policies/security-awareness-and-training-policy.md) | Completion tracking and role-based curriculum requirements. At Phase 2, a Tier 2 policy adds EU AI Act Article 14 human oversight training obligations for staff interacting with High-Risk AI systems. | ISO 27001:2022 A.6.3, NIST SP 800-50 |
