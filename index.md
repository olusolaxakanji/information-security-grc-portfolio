## What This Is

This repository is a live GRC practitioner workshop — a complete, audit-ready governance programme built from scratch and documented to the standards I apply in real-world work.

The portfolio is structured around a deliberate case study: Emyzer Technology, a fictional multinational, acquires an AI-native startup (Veridian AI) and navigates the governance consequences — EU AI Act obligations on inherited AI systems, M&A policy integration, GDPR data processor gaps, and the challenge of maintaining compliance continuity through material organisational change. The work is fictional in name; the problems it solves are real.

**What you'll find here:** 10 enterprise security policies, a full risk programme, an ISO 22301 Business Continuity Plan, an EU AI Act article-by-article control mapping, M&A GRC integration documentation, AI system risk assessments, an AI vendor due diligence addendum, operational evidence records, and a compliance mapping across all frameworks. Every artefact includes a defined evidence index, framework mapping, and honest documentation of open gaps — because governance without audit trail is not governance.

## The Case Study

**Phase 1 — Emyzer Technology** is a fictional multinational technology company: laptop production, cloud service management, enterprise technical support, and global security implementation across Asia, the Americas, Africa, and Europe. Phase 1 builds the foundational GRC programme from the ground up — policies, risk methodology, business continuity, and a controls library implemented in ServiceNow GRC.

**Acquisition** — Emyzer Technology acquires Veridian AI, an AI-native startup with two production AI systems: a Predictive Customer Churn Model (PCM-001) and an AI-Assisted Contract Review Tool (CRT-001, powered by a third-party LLM API). Neither system had been assessed against EU AI Act requirements. Neither had a data processing agreement in place for its external API calls. Both were in production.

**Phase 2 — Emyzer Nexus** is the post-acquisition parent entity. The Phase 2 programme addresses the governance challenges that make M&A integration genuinely difficult: which entity's policies govern, how inherited AI systems are brought into EU AI Act compliance, what GDPR obligations attach to training data collected by the acquired company, and how Phase 1 documentation remains in effect without creating compliance gaps.

This is the problem most GRC portfolios never address. The organisational structure below shows the full picture:

```
Emyzer Nexus  (parent — Phase 2 governance)
├── Emyzer Technology  (subsidiary — Phase 1 documentation in effect)
└── Veridian AI  (acquired — governance integration in progress)
```

## Live Nonprofit GRC Program — Idowu Ajiri Foundation

**[View the programme repository →](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/idowu-ajiri-grc-program)**

The Idowu Ajiri Foundation GRC programme is a live, operational governance programme — built and maintained by me in my role as GRC Analyst for the Foundation. Unlike the Emyzer Technology case study, this is real work for a real organisation.

The Foundation operates under dual US-Nigeria jurisdiction: incorporated as a tax-exempt entity under [IRS 501(c)(3)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits) in the United States and registered with the [Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)](https://www.cac.gov.ng) in Nigeria. Its cross-border operations create AML/CFT obligations under [SCUML](https://www.nfiu.gov.ng) — the Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering, supervised by the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit — data protection obligations under the [Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA)](https://ndpc.gov.ng), and fund flow screening obligations under [OFAC](https://ofac.treas.gov). Governing a dual-jurisdiction nonprofit — where US federal tax-exempt compliance, Nigerian corporate law, AML/CFT registration, and data protection obligations all intersect — is a materially different class of governance problem from single-jurisdiction enterprise GRC. The programme was built from the ground up to address all applicable frameworks simultaneously.

### AI Governance

**[Browse the ai-governance directory →](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/tree/main/ai-governance)**

The centrepiece of the Phase 2 programme. Two Veridian AI systems — a churn prediction model (PCM-001) and an LLM-based contract review tool (CRT-001) — were inherited at acquisition without EU AI Act conformity assessments. This directory documents the full governance response.

The EU AI Act control mapping covers Articles 9–15, 72, and 73 with a compliance status, evidence reference, and gap assessment for each obligation against each system. Two risks are rated Critical at assessment close: LLM hallucination risk (score 15) and the absent GDPR Article 28 DPA for CRT-001's external API (score 16).

**[Browse the veridian-ai directory →](https://github.com/olusolaxakanji/information-security-grc-portfolio/tree/main/veridian-ai)**

It covers model transparency requirements, training data provenance, bias and fairness testing, EU AI Act compliance status, contractual safeguards for AI error liability, and model continuity obligations. CRT-001's LLM API vendor is the first relationship subject to it.

## Competencies Demonstrated

**AI governance and EU AI Act** — Applied formal EU AI Act risk tier classification to real-world AI system types. Built article-by-article compliance mapping. Designed controls that address the specific failure modes of those systems — model drift, LLM hallucination, training data bias, prompt injection — not generic policy language.

**M&A GRC integration** — Structured a post-acquisition governance programme that resolves the actual hard problem: policy hierarchy on Day 0, inherited compliance gaps, and maintaining continuity through organisational change. Documented decisions, not just intentions.

**Risk programme design** — End-to-end risk methodology: scoring matrix, asset register with 15 operational entries, and a risk appetite statement with domain-specific thresholds. The register documents a budget-blocked Critical risk openly. That is how real risk registers should work.

**Framework translation** — Mapped abstract obligations (EU AI Act Articles 9–15, ISO 22301 clauses, NIST CSF functions) to operational documentation with traceable control linkages. Not checklist compliance — documented rationale for every design decision.

**GRC platform proficiency** — Built and managed policies, risk registers, and compliance workflows in ServiceNow GRC. Platform exports in `servicenow-evidence/` provide direct evidence of module-level work.

**Vendor risk management for AI** — Extended third-party risk controls into AI-specific due diligence covering model transparency, training data provenance, bias testing, and EU AI Act compliance assessment. A gap most existing TPRM programmes have not closed.

**Evidence architecture** — Every artefact carries a defined system of record, retention period, and owner. Audit readiness is built into the programme structure, not added at the end.
